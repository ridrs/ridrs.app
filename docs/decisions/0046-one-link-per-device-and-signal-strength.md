# ADR 0046: One Link per Device, Chosen by the Rider; Signal Strength of the Devices Found

**Amended by [ADR 0051](0051-same-device-over-both-links-by-advertising-data.md):** the device details show a device both links see as one box, and bridge protocol 1.2 carries the advertising data together with `rssi`.

## Context

[ADR 0042](0042-device-links-in-the-peripheral-link.md) connected a device over every link that saw it, used the data of one, and failed over to the other when the link in use was lost. That doubled the connections, the state the link keeps and the cases to test, before any rider needed it. With many devices of a kind in one room (a studio with 20 smart trainers), the rider also needs a way to tell the one in front of them from the others.

## Decision

- **One device per link.** Both links still scan. A device both see is listed once per link, each a device of its own with that link's id (a link's id that the other link already uses gets the link's name in front, as ids name devices alone). Matching the two by name failed on devices that send their name late, and a single-connection device stops advertising while one link holds it, so the other link could only offer it by chance. The first device seen directly connects; one only the companion saw waits 2 s for the direct link first. A lost link reconnects over the same link with backoff; ridrs never switches links on its own.
- **The rider switches the link by connecting the other device.** `device.connect-requested` of the device found over the other link lets the first one go and connects that one (the device setup keeps both links scanning while it is open). Each device found shows its link: a Bluetooth icon for this device, a phone icon for the companion, and every Connect names its link ("Connect with Bluetooth", "Connect with companion"); the current device seen over the other link offers "Switch to" that link ("Switch to companion", "Switch to Bluetooth") next to Remove. The setup offers nothing with the companion while ridrs is not connected to it: its devices found are left out, so nothing waits on a phone that is not there. Outside an open setup, a link with a device stops scanning.
- **Forget instead of Disconnect.** `device.forget-requested` replaces `device.disconnect-requested`, which kept the kind without a device until the rider connected one. The device is let go and no device of the kind connects on its own, neither while the device setup stays open nor once it closes; the next time a device setup opens, the best device found connects on its own again: one seen directly first, then the strongest signal. A first version connected another device of the kind found in its place while the setup was open, which picked a second device the rider did not want.
- **Removed:** the second connection, the failover, the choice of whose data is used, `connectedRoutes` in `device.connection-changed` and `device.status-changed`, and `device.route-requested` with the link choice in the device details (recordings that carry them still parse and replay; the field and the event are ignored).
- **Signal strength.** `BleDiscoveredPeripheral` and `DeviceCandidate` carry an optional `rssi` in dBm: `tauri-plugin-blec` (`BleDevice.rssi`), noble (`peripheral.rssi`) and the companion (bridge protocol 1.2: `rssi` in `discovered`, only to a ridrs of 1.2 or later) report it. `SharedBleScan` reports a device again once its signal moved by 5 dB or more, so the advertisements the platforms report many times a second do not become an input each. The device details list the devices found strongest first when they open, and keep that order while open (signals jitter; moving rows would be hard to hit), each with four signal bars (no dBm: the bars are enough to find the nearest).

## Consequences

- No live data of devices found but not connected: power and cadence come only over a connection. Briefly connecting to the strongest few to show their data ("pedal to find yours") stays open until a rider needs it.
- A ridrs with protocol 1.1 would reject `rssi` (its messages are strict), so a companion with 1.2 sends it none: its devices found show no signal bars. The companion and ridrs can be released apart.
- Switching links means connecting a device that stopped advertising while the other link held it. Android forgets every device when a scan starts, so blec then scans for the address itself, and a `SharedBleScan` restart would end that scan: the device was never found, the link reconnected forever, and the restarts got the companion throttled ("scanning too frequently"). `TauriBleCentral.connect` therefore runs under `SharedBleScan.pausedWhile`: no scan of ours from the moment it starts until blec is done.
- Some devices send their name only in the scan response (macOS reported the Xiaomi Smart Band 10 nameless at first). `SharedBleScan` reports a device again once its name arrives, and the link gives the device that name.
- A device both links see is two devices found of one name; the link icons tell them apart (one box since ADR 0051). Forgetting one connects no device of the kind on its own until the device setup opens again; the rider can connect the other one.
