# ADR 0089: A Device Made of Several Parts; the Zwift Ride Pair as One Device

Status: accepted on 2026-11-12 (the pair as one device, chosen by the maintainer) and implemented the same day. Amends [ADR 0042](0042-device-links-in-the-peripheral-link.md) and [ADR 0046](0046-one-link-per-device-and-signal-strength.md) (a device of parts is offered and connected as one), [ADR 0054](0054-plugin-settings-and-remembered-devices.md) (one remembered entry for all parts, `device-controller-2` merged once) and [ADR 0069](0069-recording-devices-and-bridge-beside-the-bus.md) (`device.status` gains `batteries` and `missingParts`, `battery` is gone).

## Context

The Zwift Ride is two BLE devices, the left and the right controller, with the same name and service. `device-zwift-ride` ran two independent links that shared the controllers out (`accept`), each with its own key of `device.status` and its own settings document (`device-controller`, `device-controller-2`); only the first link decoded, since every link sees all raw packets of its plugin, and the buttons of each controller were published under its own id. The device setup needed exceptions for it (`kind === 'controller'`: no "Switch to", no devices found), and its proof merged the buttons of all controllers. A rider could end up with one controller of each of two sets, with no hint that one was missing, and Remove let go of only one of them.

The maintainer decided that the driver presents the pair as one device.

**What the advertising data tells.** Nothing in the repository captured a Zwift Ride's advertisements: `experiments/hr-monitor-osx/zwift-packet-capture.log` holds notifications only (its LEFT and RIGHT lines carry the same peripheral id, so it most likely heard one controller twice), the simulators advertised no data for it, and `advertising.ts` only compares data, it reads no field. The open-source SwiftControl project reads Zwift's manufacturer data (company id 0x094A): its first byte is the device type, 0x08 for the left and 0x07 for the right Zwift Ride controller (0x03 and 0x02 for the Zwift Play). Nothing known in the advertisements tells which controllers belong to one set. This is not yet checked against ridrs' own hardware.

## Decision

- **A building block for devices made of several parts**: `startBlePartsLink` in `@ridrs/ble-peripheral`, generic over the parts (`parts: ['left', 'right']`) and a plugin's `partOf(peripheral)`, which reads the part from the advertising data or says nothing. One link per part runs the lifecycle of `startBlePeripheralLink` (`createLink`, which now reports to a port: the status of a device of one part, or the device of parts), so scanning, the direct link first, reconnecting, the handshake, battery and `sameDeviceAs` stay one implementation. On top it keeps:
  - **One status**, one key of `device.status` (`<plugin>/<kind>/1`). The state is `connected` only once every part is; while some parts are connected and others are not it is `connecting`, or `reconnecting` once a part was lost or fails, and the status names the parts it waits for: `missingParts` (`DevicePart[]`). The UI turns it into a tip from its own texts (`waitingFor`, `parts`, en and de); it knows parts, not controllers.
  - **One connection state per change** (`device.connection-changed`, an input) under the device's id, with the id of each part found (`parts: [{ part, deviceId }]`). The parts' links publish no states of their own.
  - **Devices found are devices of parts**: per link, the strongest device found becomes a device with the strongest device of each other part (a device that says its part beats one that does not), and so on; a part found alone is a device with one part. No part is ever offered alone. The signal of a device of parts is that of its farthest part; `sameDeviceAs` holds when one of its parts is a part of the device over the other link.
  - **Connect and forget take every part.** Connecting a device found connects each of its parts on a link of its own; a link without a part found takes the best one found that fits, as with nothing remembered. Forget lets every part go.
  - **Which link takes which device**: a link takes the part it remembers; otherwise a device no other link holds or remembers, of a part no other link holds or remembers. With two sets in the room the first choice is thus the strongest left and the strongest right controller (once both are found; otherwise the first found of each side). The side a link stands for is that of its device's advertising, else the remembered one, else its place.
  - **One remembered entry**: settings document `device-<kind>` version 2 (`rememberedPartsSchema`): `{ state: 'chosen', deviceId, parts: [{ part?, route, peripheralId, name }] }` or `{ state: 'forgotten' }`. It stays until the rider forgets the device; choosing the parts is needed only when pairing.
  - **Raw inputs keep the part's id**; the device decodes them once, in the turn of the input, live and on replay, and gives the plugin the device and the part (`DevicePartOf`), mapped through the `parts` of its own connection inputs.
- **The device's id is the id of the part found first** (the one its link connected or the rider chose first), kept in the remembered entry. It is an id ridrs already has (no new scheme, nothing from randomness, rule 7), unique (a controller belongs to one pair), the same in the devices found and once connected, so the rider's choice does not change it, and the same after a restart because it is remembered. A composite of both ids was rejected: it does not exist while one controller is away, and ids from links may contain any character (macOS ids in base64 contain `+` and `/`). A generated id was rejected: it would not reproduce on replay without being an input.
- **`batteries` replaces `battery`** in `device.status`: `[{ part?, level }]`, one entry without `part` for a device of one part, one per part that told it otherwise. The device setup shows the weakest on the card and, for a device of parts, every part's level among the facts ("Left battery", "Right battery"; "Akku links", "Akku rechts").
- **Zwift Ride**: `device-zwift-ride` runs `startBlePartsLink` with the parts `left` and `right`; its `partOf` reads the first byte of manufacturer data `094a` (`ZWIFT_MANUFACTURER_ID`, `ZWIFT_RIDE_LEFT`, `ZWIFT_RIDE_RIGHT` in the contracts, for the simulator too). The buttons it publishes are those held on either controller, under the pair's id: a button both controllers tell (the left one forwards the right one's) counts once. A pair that is not connected any more releases them; the controller still there tells its buttons again.
- **Migration**: `loadDevicePartsMemory(ctx, 'device-controller', ['device-controller-2'])` reads version 2; a document of version 1 is migrated, and then, once, the documents of the further links from before are read and merged: the controllers any of them chose, in link order, the device's id that of the first, or "forgotten" when none chose one. The merged entry is written at once; from then on the old documents are left alone (nothing deletes settings documents yet). An unreadable old document is a `settings-invalid` diagnostic, as before, and never stops the start.
- **Recordings from before the pair** carry the controllers' raw packets and their connection states under their own ids, without `parts`. On replay each controller then stands for itself: its buttons are published under its own id, as they were recorded, and the plugins after it (`control-virtual-gears`, `control-erg`) keep their held buttons per device id, so they act as before. The replay is identical (`zwift-ride-controller.feature`, rule "Recordings from before the pair").
- **The UI** lost its pair exceptions: devices found and "Switch to" for the controller as for every kind; the controller's proof shows the buttons of the controller shown only (`Readings.buttons` holds one device's buttons, as power and heart rate do).

## Consequences

- A rider with one controller switched on sees the controller row connecting, with the tip to wake the other side; Remove lets go of both.
- With two sets in a room, the first choice may take controllers of different sets, the strongest of each side: nothing tells sets apart. The rider can connect another pair found; the choice is remembered.
- Without advertising data (an older companion protocol, a platform that does not report it), the sides are taken by order: the first controller found is the left one. The tip and the battery labels may then name the wrong side; connecting, buttons and memory do not depend on it.
- The side bytes come from SwiftControl, not from ridrs' own captures: a capture of the advertisements with the real pair is still to do. If the bytes differ, only `partOf` changes; the pair still works without sides.
- The old settings document `device-controller-2` stays on disk unused.
- `device-setup-proof.feature` no longer shows the buttons of two controllers together: a pair is one device, and another controller's buttons do not prove the one shown.
- Out of scope: several kinds per device, the power row, a cadence driver, the companion as a device, new rules for which rows are shown.
